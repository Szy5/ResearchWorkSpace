\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}
\vspace{-0.10in}

\textbf{Abstention as a capability.}
Prior work defines abstention as refusing to directly answer a query: refusing explicitly, offering multiple perspectives, expressing ignorance, or communicating uncertainty~\citep{wen2025knowlimits}. Benchmarks such as \textsc{Abstain-QA}~\citep{madhusudhan2024llmsknowanswerinvestigating}, SelfAware~\citep{yin2023largelanguagemodelsknow}, and AbstentionBench~\citep{kirichenko2025abstentionbench} evaluate this capability; AbstentionBench is the most directly related, with 35k unanswerable questions across 6 scenarios showing that scaling and chain-of-thought do not solve the problem. Calibration work argues abstention must be \emph{taught} via fine-tuning or explicit uncertainty signals~\citep{kapoor2025largelanguagemodelstaught,cohen2024idontknowexplicit,tomani2024uncertaintybasedabstentionllmsimproves,wu2026mitigatingllmhallucinationbehaviorally}. The converse is equally severe: OR-Bench~\citep{cui2025orbench} and XSTest~\citep{rottger2024xstest} show that safety training triggers over-refusal, and FalseReject~\citep{zhang2025falsereject} provides paired training signals for this calibration. These results establish act/abstain calibration as a two-sided trade-off, motivating our paired design. Abstention has also been instantiated in specific agent modalities: FixedBench~\citep{gloaguen2026codingagents} tests whether coding agents refrain from patching already-resolved issues, and embodied benchmarks evaluate answer-or-abstain decisions in embodied QA~\citep{wu2025abstaineqa} and robotic instruction following~\citep{yeke2026yesman}; each covers a single trigger type or modality rather than a systematic scenario space. Recent work such as HiL-Bench~\citep{elfeki2026hil} focuses on one abstention form (escalation under incomplete specifications, comparable to our S1/S2); \benchmark{} adds seven further scenarios spanning pre-execution and runtime triggers, with both refusal and clarification requests counting as valid abstention.

\textbf{Agent safety, clarification, and reliable tool use.}
Work on clarification studies when agents should \emph{ask} rather than act. Noisy-ToolBench~\citep{wang2026learningaskllmagents} perturbs tool-use queries with missing information and tool infeasibility, finding that agents fabricate arguments rather than ask; AskBench~\citep{zhao2026askaskbenchrubricguidedrlvr} trains this decision via RLVR. These clarification benchmarks, however, focus on pre-execution decisions and cannot surface failures that emerge mid-trajectory. On the tool-use side, ToolBeHonest~\citep{zhang2024toolbehonest} formalizes solvability detection and reports that frontier models score only 37--45\% on recognizing unsolvable tasks, but evaluates agents \emph{statically} over a fixed tool set and covers only tool-level unsolvability. Safety benchmarks such as AgentHarm~\citep{andriushchenko2025agentharm} and Agent-SafetyBench~\citep{zhang2025agentsafetybench} focus on safety-driven refusal, rather than calibrated abstention from legitimate tasks that become problematic during execution.

\textbf{Positioning.}
Two gaps emerge. First, prior abstention research remains confined to textual QA and, more recently, single-scenario instantiations in coding and embodied settings; no prior benchmark extends it to tool-using agents whose halt decisions are grounded in environment states and carry real side effects. Second, current agent benchmarks each isolate one slice of restraint (pre-execution clarification, static unsolvability, safety refusal, or deception detection) without jointly addressing \emph{when} a halt is required and \emph{what consequences} blind execution would produce. \benchmark{} bridges both gaps as the first benchmark to jointly evaluate agentic abstention along three axes: \emph{phase} (pre-execution vs.\ runtime triggers), \emph{side effects}, and \emph{paired counterfactual design}.

Concurrent work has begun to study the same problem: \citet{luo2026agentic} adapt WebShop, Terminal-Bench, and AbstentionBench to study when agents stop acting, and a position paper makes the case for abstention-aware agent evaluation~\citep{ojewale2026benchmarks}. \benchmark{} differs in generating rather than adapting its environments and tasks, in runtime-trigger coverage beyond missing targets and prerequisites (high-stakes actions, tool failure, conflicting evidence, emergent risk), and in scoring both sides of each pair while distinguishing verbal refusal from withheld execution, the properties that make failure modes such as post-hoc abstention measurable.
