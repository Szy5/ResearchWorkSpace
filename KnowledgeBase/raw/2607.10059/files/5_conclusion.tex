\section{Conclusion}
\vspace{-0.10in}

We presented the first systematic evaluation framework for agentic abstention: the calibrated ability of tool-using LLM agents to recognize when not to act, spanning both pre-execution reasoning and runtime discovery. The framework has two components. The benchmark, \benchmark{}, instantiates an agent-native taxonomy of 8 abstention scenarios as 263 paired tasks across 42 executable sandbox environments. The pipeline, \pipeline{}, scales those environments and paired tasks end-to-end, so fresh instances can be regenerated on demand as models evolve. Across 17 frontier models, agentic abstention remains an open problem: the best agent reaches only 59.5\% paired accuracy, and agents systematically prioritize acting over abstaining. More importantly, abstention capability is largely independent of general task-solving capability, implying that scaling task-solving alone will not close this gap; calibrated restraint must be cultivated as a distinct training objective. We release \benchmark{} and \pipeline{} as foundations towards building agent systems that are not only capable but also trustworthy in knowing their limits.
